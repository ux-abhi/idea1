The glass droplet

Where Pip has room, it shows its whole body: a droplet of tinted glass with light drifting inside. The particles change colour and movement with Pip’s mood, and the eyes do the talking.

  • App icon, setup conversation, the Pip panel in the Mac app, the floating Pip button on iPhone
  • In the notch and the Dynamic Island the black hardware is the body, so only the eyes and the inner light appear
  • Same state machine everywhere. The chips above drive this droplet too

Review of your sketch

What works, and what I changed

Keep
  • The tilted pill eyes. They are the most ownable part. They read as alive at 7 pixels wide, so they work in the notch.
  • The droplet. A simple silhouette that stays recognisable as an app icon at any size.
  • Warm orange as Pip’s home colour. Pip glows orange when idle and switches colour by mood.
Changed
  • Two straight pills look like a pause button ⏸. The Dynamic Island already uses that symbol for music, so the eyes keep a stronger tilt, sit at slightly different heights, and change shape with each mood.
  • Flat fill becomes glass with light inside, to match Apple’s Liquid Glass look on macOS 26 and iOS 26.
  • The orange is close to Claude’s brand colour. That fits, since Claude powers Pip, but particles in other colours keep Pip its own character.
Rules

Glow inside, battery first

Inside the notch only

  • The notch clips everything: particles, colour glow and glass sheen
  • No outer shadow or halo on the screen around the notch
  • Glass is a soft top highlight plus a thin inner rim of light along the bottom edge
  • The hardware fillets where the notch meets the bezel stay pure black

Particles and battery

  • At most 30 particles, drawn at 30 frames per second, and only while Pip has something to say
  • At rest the frame freezes and only a blink plays. This page keeps it moving so you can see it
  • Calm mode stops all motion when the lid is closed, in full screen, or on battery under 20%
  • iPhone surfaces use pre-rendered still poses, never live particles
